# How to verify a startup credit offer before you apply

Cloud credits can remove a real expense from an early startup budget. An offer summary can also outlive the terms it describes. Before applying, follow a simple source rule: find the exact offer, then check the provider's own eligibility and application pages. If the two disagree, use the provider's current terms. A directory entry can help locate the clause, but it cannot approve a company or guarantee an award. Here is what that check found across three programs on 18 September 2026.

AWS Activate Founders illustrates the gap between a starting award and a ceiling. [Sourcey's dated record](https://sourcey.com/c/aws/offers/aws-activate-founders-credits), observed on 4 August 2026, lists $1,000 in AWS credits for self-funded, pre-Series B startups founded within the last ten years. Selected participants can receive up to $5,000. The [AWS credits page](https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits?lang=en-US) confirms that the Founders tier starts at $1,000 and that the higher amount applies to selected participants. It also requires an AWS account on a paid tier plan. A founder budgeting for the full $5,000 before an award arrives would be planning around the maximum rather than the initial offer.

Google Cloud's Start tier applies a much shorter company-age test. [Sourcey's Start tier record](https://sourcey.com/c/google-cloud/offers/google-for-startups-cloud-program-start), observed on 4 August 2026, describes up to $2,000 in Google Cloud credits for one year. It specifies a technology startup founded within the last 24 months, planning to seek venture funding, with no previous Google Cloud credits beyond the free trial. [Google's own Start tier page](https://cloud.google.com/startup/pre-funded) adds a working MVP and a clear business model to the selection criteria. A three-year-old bootstrapped business should check that age condition before spending time on an application, even if its infrastructure needs are exactly what the program supports.

DigitalOcean's program exposes a different kind of threshold. [Sourcey's DigitalOcean record](https://sourcey.com/c/digitalocean/offers/digitalocean-startups-program), observed on 27 July 2026, lists 12 months of cloud credits with an amount that varies, plus 15 months of Standard-tier support. It does not turn that variable amount into a single promised award. On the [DigitalOcean Startups page](https://www.digitalocean.com/startups), the provider says applicants need a company website, a matching business email address, and a DigitalOcean team account. It also says service businesses such as consultancies and development agencies are ineligible. These requirements matter more than a headline credit number to a solo founder who has not yet set up a company domain.

For offers like these, write down the exact tier and initial award separately from any "up to" figure. Then list the eligibility conditions and mark which ones your company can prove today. Check the provider's application and credit-use terms for account requirements, covered services, and expiry. Save both URLs and the date of the check; a later change in terms should not quietly become a surprise bill.

There is also a budgeting distinction. Promotional credits pay eligible charges on a provider's platform. An award may cover fewer services than a planned workload uses. For AWS, the paid account requirement affects the application path. For Google, company age and prior credit use narrow the Start tier. For DigitalOcean, a variable credit amount and company identity requirements shape the decision before price even enters it.

Consider a three-year-old development agency preparing a cloud migration. Google Cloud's Start tier fails its age test. DigitalOcean excludes service businesses. AWS Founders could still be worth checking if the company meets its funding, age, and account conditions. Ten minutes spent on the provider pages can turn a list of tempting credit figures into one realistic application and a budget based on the award actually available.
